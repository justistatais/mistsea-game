All departures remain subject to the current navigation restrictions. Route A is closed until further notice. A previous journey through that channel is not evidence that it is safe to use today, and an older chart should not be treated as permission to enter it.

`Q102-R-NOTICE-p02`
Route C remains open, but a vessel may enter it only if its navigation beacon is operational. The beacon must be checked in its installed configuration before departure. Permission to work on a vessel does not remove this requirement, nor does the fact that another vessel has already left the harbour.

`Q102-R-NOTICE-p03`
A separate notice at the dispatch desk sets out the conditions for Route B. Crews should compare those conditions with the particulars of their own vessel rather than assume that a channel available to one boat is available to every boat. The departure plan must identify the chosen route and explain any equipment arrangements on which it depends.

## LAB-R-001 · When a timetable is not a guarantee

模态：reading；约616词（制作估算）；材料家族：FAM-LAB-TIMETABLE-TRIAL。

`LAB-R-001-p01`
When the town of Alder introduced a new evening bus service, the announcement promised a better connection between its railway station and three residential districts. The published timetable showed a bus leaving ten minutes after each of the main evening trains. For passengers who had previously faced a long walk or an expensive taxi journey, the arrangement appeared straightforward. Yet during the first month, complaints arrived from people who had missed their connection even though their train had reached the station on time.

`LAB-R-001-p02`
The transport office initially looked for mistakes in the timetable. None were found. The difficulty was that the ten-minute connection had been measured from the arrival of the train at the platform to the departure of the bus from its stop. It did not include the time passengers might need to leave a crowded carriage, reach the footbridge and collect a bicycle or a large bag. What looked like a generous interval on paper was not necessarily a usable interval for everyone.

`LAB-R-001-p03`
To investigate, staff observed the route between the platform and the bus stop over five evenings. They also invited passengers to describe their journeys. The two sources did not provide identical information. Observation showed where groups slowed down, whereas the written accounts explained why some people needed to stop. A parent reported waiting for a child to use the station facilities; another passenger avoided the footbridge because the lift was unreliable. These were not errors in reading the timetable. They were conditions that the timetable alone did not reveal.

`LAB-R-001-p04`
One proposal was simply to delay every bus by another ten minutes. Several passengers supported it, but the bus operators raised a practical concern. The same vehicles continued beyond the three districts to serve a small college. A later departure from the station could make those passengers late for the final class of the evening. Changing one connection could improve a journey for one group while making another journey less reliable. The question was therefore not whether additional waiting time was useful, but where and under what conditions it should be introduced.

`LAB-R-001-p05`
The town tried a more limited change. For six weeks, the first bus after the busiest train waited an additional five minutes. The other services kept their published departure times. Staff placed signs along the walking route and asked the station to check the lift before the evening peak. This trial combined a timetable change with improvements to the connection itself. Consequently, a reduction in missed buses would not, on its own, show which part of the trial had produced the improvement.

`LAB-R-001-p06`
At the end of the trial, fewer complaints had been received. The transport office treated this as encouraging rather than decisive. A complaint count depends not only on the number of problems but also on whether passengers know where to report them and believe that reporting is worthwhile. The number of rail passengers had also varied during the trial. The office therefore compared complaints with observed missed connections and passenger numbers. It did not publish the lower complaint count as proof that every user had benefited.

`LAB-R-001-p07`
The final recommendation retained the extra wait after the busiest train and made responsibility for the lift checks explicit. It also proposed another review during the college term, when travel patterns might differ. Some residents regarded the continued review as unnecessary caution; others welcomed the acknowledgement that a useful arrangement could still need adjustment. The case suggests that a timetable is best understood as a statement of an intended service, rather than a guarantee that every person can make every connection. Assessing that service requires attention to the journey around the scheduled departure as well as to the time printed beside it.

## LAB-L-001 · A trial of later library opening

模态：listening；约603词（制作估算）；材料家族：FAM-LAB-LIBRARY-HOURS。

`LAB-L-001-p01`
Good afternoon. Today I want to explain how our college evaluated a trial of later library opening. You may have seen the announcement saying that the trial was successful. That description needs a little care. The library was used more often in the evening, but that does not answer every question about whether the change should become permanent. I will go through what changed, what we measured and what we still need to find out.

`LAB-L-001-p02`
First, the practical arrangement. Before the trial, the library closed at six on weekdays. During the trial it stayed open until eight, but only on Tuesdays and Thursdays. We did not add weekend hours. The original proposal had included Monday as well, and some early notices referred to three evenings. However, there were not enough trained staff to cover all three. Anyone using those early notices would have had the wrong information, so the final timetable was placed beside the entrance and sent to students.

`LAB-L-001-p03`
The aim was not simply to increase the total number of visits. Students on placement had told us that they often returned to campus too late to use specialist materials. Other students said their accommodation was too noisy for concentrated work. Those are different needs. A student might require access to a particular collection, or might only need a quiet desk. We wanted to know which need the extra hours were actually meeting before deciding how to use the available staff and space.

`LAB-L-001-p04`
For eight weeks, the team recorded entrances during the extra hours. They also asked visitors to complete a short form when they left. I should stress that the entrance figures were visits, not separate individuals. Someone who came on six evenings appeared six times in the total. It would therefore be misleading to report the total as the number of students who had benefited. The forms helped us see patterns of use, although not everyone chose to complete one.

`LAB-L-001-p05`
The most common reason given on the forms was the need for a quiet place to work. Access to specialist materials was mentioned less often. That finding led one member of the team to suggest opening a supervised study room instead of the whole library. It might meet much of the demand at a lower staffing cost. But another member pointed out that a less common need can still be important. A small group may depend on equipment or collections that cannot be moved into an ordinary room.

`LAB-L-001-p06`
We also compared the two evenings. Thursday was usually busier than Tuesday, but the difference became smaller after the deadline for a major course assignment had passed. We cannot assume that students always prefer Thursday. Nor can we say that later opening improved examination results: the trial did not measure them. Increased use is useful evidence about access, not direct evidence about academic achievement. When you read the short report, keep that distinction in mind.

`LAB-L-001-p07`
Our recommendation is to keep the two late evenings for one more term while testing a supervised study room on another day. This is a proposed next step, not an approved timetable, so students should not arrive next Monday expecting the new room to be open. Before it starts, the college needs to confirm staffing and access arrangements. We will compare costs, types of use and the experience of students who need specialist resources. We also plan to ask people who did not attend the trial, because a service can look popular while still failing to reach some of those it was intended to help. That wider review should give us a better basis for a permanent decision.
